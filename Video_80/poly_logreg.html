2 · What is happening

Plain logistic regression (d = 1) can only draw a straight boundary, so it underfits curved data. Raising d adds curved terms (x₁², x₂², x₁³, …), each with its own learned weight — the model stays logistic regression, but its boundary can now bend. 2 · What is happening

Plain logistic regression (d = 1) can only draw a straight boundary, so it underfits curved data. Raising d adds curved terms (x₁², x₂², x₁³, …), each with its own learned weight — the model stays logistic regression, but its boundary can now bend. Too much bending and the boundary starts wiggling around individual points instead of following the true U: that is overfitting, and its accuracy on new data drops even though the training score keeps looking good.
