A low-degree curve is too stiff to follow the bend — it misses the data in a systematic way (high bias, underfit). A very high-degree curve twists to hit nearly every training dot, including the noise — so it swings wildly between dots and fails on new test points (high variance, overfit). The sweet spot sits in the middle. This tension is the bias–variance tradeoff, the topic of the very next video.
