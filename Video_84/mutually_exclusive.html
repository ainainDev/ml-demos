🎲 Mutually Exclusive vs Independent Events Day 84 — Naive Bayes, Part 3 · drag the sliders and watch P(A∩B) and P(A|B) respond

Mutually exclusive: P(A∩B) = 0  ·  P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B) = 0
Independent: P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B)  ·  P(A|B) = P(A)

Venn diagram — watch the circles separate or overlap

P(A∩B) –
P(A|B) –
Mode Mutually exclusive

When events are mutually exclusive, knowing B happened tells you A did not happen — so P(A|B) = 0 no matter how you move the sliders. For independent events, knowing B happened tells you nothing new about A, so P(A|B) just equals P(A). This contrast (from Video 83) is the probability groundwork Bayes' theorem builds on next.
