🌳 Bagging vs Random Forest — feel the one extra trick

Both panels train an ensemble of decision stumps (one-split trees) on the same 2-D dataset, where the true boundary is the diagonal x₁ + x₂ = 10. The only difference is the video's key idea, shrunk to miniature: the bagging stumps may split on any feature, while each Random Forest stump must split on one randomly picked feature.

What to notice
  • The Random Forest boundary hugs the diagonal with a finer staircase — many stumps splitting on x₁ and many on x₂ blend into the diagonal shape.
  • Trees disagreeing = average share of stumps voting against the ensemble majority on the test set. Random Forest's number is higher — its trees are decorrelated, and decorrelated errors cancel out in the vote.
  • Bagging stumps mostly pick the same "best" feature and threshold, so the ensemble barely beats a single stump.
  • In the video the trees are deep and Random Forest re-samples columns before every node split (not just once per tree). Here each tree is a single stump, so the same idea appears once per stump — the miniature version of the real mechanism.
