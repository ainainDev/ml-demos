🤖 Wisdom of the Crowd: Ensembles Average Away the Noise Day 101 — K weak straight-line learners, each trained on a bootstrap sample of noisy data

Idea: the true curve is y = sin(x) (dashed white). Each blue line is one weak learner — an ordinary least-squares fit trained on a different bootstrap sample (random resample, with replacement) of the noisy dots. One learner wobbles with its sample's noise; the green bold line is the ensemble's average prediction. Drag the slider up and watch the average steady onto the truth.

Each faint line is one learner — the bold green line is their average

Each learner is closed-form least squares: slope = Cov(x,y) / Var(x), intercept = ȳ − slope·x̄, fitted on its own bootstrap sample. RMSE is measured against the true sin(x) curve, not the noisy dots — so "better" means closer to the truth. The ensemble always reduces squared error because averaging cancels the individual mistakes: (mean fᵢ − y)² ≤ mean (fᵢ − y)².
