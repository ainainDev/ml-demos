📦 The Curse of Dimensionality — Day 46

Two interactive panels that show why machine learning gets harder as the number of features grows.

(a) Exponential Data Hunger

Drag the slider. To keep the same density of data as in 1-D, the number of points needed grows as 5d.


Points needed to keep density: 25

(b) Distance Concentration

Pick a dimension. We draw 200 random points inside a unit box and plot the histogram of all pairwise distances.

Watch the histogram get narrower as d grows — every point becomes almost equally far from every other point.

What this shows: In high dimensions, data becomes sparse — you need exponentially more samples just to fill the space (panel a). Worse, distances concentrate: the nearest neighbor is almost as far as the farthest one (panel b). Lost-wallet intuition: imagine losing your wallet in a small room vs. a 100-room mansion — in the mansion, everywhere is "far away", so "nearest" stops meaning anything. That is why distance-based methods like KNN struggle in very high dimensions.
