Verdict –

The "naive" part is right there in the formula: we multiply the word likelihoods as if every word were independent of the others. Words aren't truly independent ("free" and "money" love to travel together), but the simplification works surprisingly well in practice. Unknown words are skipped — the classifier only judges words it has seen. Try "lets meet for lunch tomorrow" for the ham side.
