💠 Why Lasso creates sparsity

Two views of the same fact. Top: the L1 diamond (blue) shrinks as λ grows; the loss contours (ellipses) touch it at a corner — and corners sit on an axis, where one coefficient is exactly zero. Bottom: the soft-thresholding formula for one coefficient, with its λ-wide dead zone shaded.

1 · Constraint geometry

The red × is the unpenalized (least-squares) optimum. The cyan ellipse is the smallest loss contour that still touches the diamond — that touching point is the Lasso solution. Drag λ and watch it stay pinned to the corner.

2 · Soft-thresholding: m = sign(num)·max(|num|−λ, 0) / den

One coefficient, den = 2. The dashed line is λ = 0 (plain least squares). The shaded band is the dead zone: any signal with |num| ≤ λ is clamped to exactly 0. Ridge has no dead zone — its curve is smooth through the origin.
