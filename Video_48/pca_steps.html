Step 3 — Eigendecomposition: A·v = λ·v

Find the directions v that the covariance matrix A only stretches: A·v = λ·v. The stretch factor λ is the variance along that direction.

Step 4 — Project onto PC1: Z = X·V

Take the dot product of every (centered) point with the top eigenvector — each point becomes one number.

Z = X · V  (X centered, V = top eigenvector)

What this shows: PCA in four mechanical steps — center the data, measure spread with the covariance matrix, find the natural axes of that spread (eigenvectors) and how stretched each is (eigenvalues), then project every point onto the top axis. One number per point now carries … of the original variance — that is dimensionality reduction.
